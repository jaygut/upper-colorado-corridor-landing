Three testable properties

A name the land has to earn.

Markets for nature tend to price land by the hectare and carbon by the tonne, yet the value that reaches a water utility, a grower or an insurer is produced by what a parcel connects. This forested strip above the Eagle River once topped a screening list, and so far that is the whole of its record, so to earn the name it would have to pass three tests, each of which can be settled with evidence gathered on the ground.

The quantitative framework · what is computed

Behind these figures, a quantitative framework.

Keystone Polygon and Spillover Effect are the two working terms of a quantitative framework that Graph of Life runs on public data. It represents a landscape as a network of places and the flows between them, traces how a function or a risk would travel along those flows, tests its predictions against observations held back from fitting wherever a record exists, and turns what survives into proposals for monitoring and, once rights and payers are named, for payment. Most of the figures in this story were computed by that framework from public data, the rest are published findings or observations still to be made, and each scene that presents evidence carries a tag that says which is which.

The unseen engine · what the record has yet to reach Pending

Most of what lives here has never been recorded.

Birds are the only organisms in the reviewed public record, and yet much of the life that keeps a forest like this one working is smaller, quieter and far closer to the ground. Bacteria and fungi in the soil help regulate how nutrients cycle, how much carbon the ground keeps and how readily water soaks in, while bees, flies and other pollinators carry the forest’s influence out to the meadows, gardens and fields within their flight range. On this parcel neither group has ever been sampled, which is why the markers on the terrain show where a first field season would take soil cores, walk a pollinator transect and count birds.

Every marker is a proposed location laid out along the parcel’s own long axis, and sampling on federal land may require a permit. The living network · published ecology Published elsewhere

A forest is a network of exchanges.

Trees, fungi, bacteria, flowers and insects trade with one another all the time. Sugars made in the canopy flow down into roots and the fungi that live on them, nitrogen and phosphorus flow back up, fungal threads bind the soil into crumbs that hold water, and in the few plants tested so far the fungi on understory roots changed how often pollinators visited, while about three-quarters of wild bee species raise their young in the soil. On the wing · published pollination ecology Published elsewhere

Pollinators carry the forest beyond its edge.

A patch of natural habitat sends its influence outward on the wings of the insects that nest in it, and how far that influence reaches depends on how far those insects fly. Small solitary bees rarely forage more than a few hundred metres from home, larger bees range further, and the studies that pooled crop data from five continents found that pollinator visits fade with distance in a strikingly regular way. Drag the slider to move outward from the parcel and watch the published curve and the ring on the terrain move together.

Nobody has yet recorded a pollinator on this parcel or mapped a flowering crop or garden within these rings, so the curve shows what published studies would lead one to look for and the rings show where that search would happen. The pooled declines were shallower in temperate regions than in the tropics, and their effect on crop yield was less clear than their effect on visits.

Stepping stones · published connectivity science Illustrative

A well-placed patch can hold a whole network together.

Animals, seeds and pollen rarely cross a valley in a single leap, and they travel instead from patch to patch, which is why network ecologists draw a landscape as a graph whose nodes are habitat patches and whose edges are the gaps a disperser can cross. In that picture a patch of sufficient size sitting between two larger ones is called a stepping stone, and when it disappears the patches on either side can drift apart, leaving the species that relied on that crossing without a route. Remove nodes from the network below, or shorten the distance a disperser can travel, and watch how connectivity responds.

Corridors, a related way of joining patches, raised movement between them by about 50% across 78 experiments, and in a landscape-scale experiment they also facilitated pollination and seed dispersal. Whether a parcel of 4.2 ha is large enough to serve as a bridge of this kind, and for which species, or whether it is one tile in a continuous forest floor, is a question that a connectivity map of the surrounding habitat would settle.

Landscape network · computed in your browser Computed · live

The parcel is one node in a connected landscape.

One trace shows one route, and a landscape holds thousands of them. Here the same rule has been run from every one of the thousands of cells of 30 m that lie within 2 km of the parcel, so that each cell becomes a node, the parcel among them, its step downhill becomes an edge, and the flow lines thicken as they gather more ground. Ecologists have a name for a set of ecosystems joined by flows of energy, materials and organisms across their boundaries, a meta-ecosystem, and the terrain shows where the water layer of such a system would run here. On this surface the parcel is a source node, with almost no ground lying upslope of it, and the route that leaves it meets the drainage of the valley within a kilometre, where the rest of this ground also arrives.

The spillover ledger · who carries the cost Published elsewhere

When a forest stops working, someone downstream pays.

Follow those lines far enough and they reach people who will never set foot in the forest they start from. Water utilities pay to remove what an unhealthy watershed sends them, reservoir operators pay to dredge what a burned slope delivers, growers rely on insects that nest in nearby habitat, and the Eagle River joins a Colorado River system that supplies water to tens of millions of people. Switch between what published studies have shown elsewhere and what has been measured for this parcel, and the gap between the two views becomes the clearest argument for the next observation.

Each finding comes from a different place, scale and disturbance, so none can be transferred to this parcel as a number. Together they show which recipients a Keystone Polygon could matter to, and which evidence would have to be gathered before any of them could be named.

Separate cases · straight-line distance Computed

The water record lives elsewhere.

So far the parcel is rich in hypotheses and poor in measurements, which is the usual condition of land that markets are asked to value. To see what evidence looks like once it exists, and how hard it is to earn, the story has to leave Eagle County. Dated water measurements exist at North Inlet and East Inlet near Grand Lake, about 100 km to the north-east, and on the Colorado River above Glenwood Springs, about 79 km to the west.

Delivery from the parcel to any of these stations is still untraced and untested. The Glenwood station does lie downstream on the Colorado River, although position on a map establishes no effect, so the chapters that follow test the method on each station's own ground.

Grand Lake, Colorado · associated basins Computed

Two basins, one fire perimeter.

Above Grand Lake two neighbouring basins drain toward two gauges, and the mapped perimeter of the East Troublesome fire reaches into one of them. That final perimeter overlaps 27.3% of the basin associated with the North Inlet station, an area of 20.6 km², and has no mapped overlap with the East Inlet basin. Land outside the perimeter stays unclassified for this fire, and imagery is lit only inside the two basins.

All of this is retrospective context, since the post-fire image dates from 7 Sep 2021 and the perimeter was revised on 16 Oct 2026, both after the August 2021 storm examined next. A spectral class records how the surface looked to a sensor, leaving mortality, recovery and biodiversity change for field measurement.

Retrospective evaluation · frozen before the final years were opened Tested

Six hours ahead.

Signals like these invite a harder test, which is whether the next few hours can be foreseen. A model fitted on 2021–2022 predicted the highest turbidity of the following six hours throughout 2023–2024, and at North Inlet it lowered the mean absolute log error by 6.84% across 1,014 windows.

  • HeldNorth Inlet accuracy improved in both final years.
  • FailedTransfer to East Inlet with the 12-input turbidity and season model, where error rose across 1,481 windows.
  • FailedPromotion of the learned monitoring policy at North Inlet and at Glenwood.
  • FailedThe earlier 48-hour storm comparator, on 13 development and 15 final storms.
  • FailedThe legacy spatial benchmark, where controls alone reached grouped R² 0.4035 and adding mechanism features lowered it to 0.2703.

The model that improved reads 19 inputs describing turbidity, discharge, rainfall and season, and the East Inlet and Glenwood models read 12 describing turbidity and season. Vegetation, habitat and species are absent from all of them, so the result speaks to those inputs on that task and leaves the importance of forests and biodiversity as an open ecological question.

Back to the parcel · evidence to obligation Pending

The last edge is a promise to pay.

The measured cases show how much work stands between a signal and a decision, and the parcel still has that work ahead of it. Back above the Eagle River, the final link is the one that turns evidence into care for the land. A demonstrated dependency becomes an obligation only when someone is named, holds the authority to commit funds and accepts a payment rule, and each of those gates opens separately, so sound evidence can sit beside unresolved rights for a long time.

Stage names describe the framework, and any accounting, insurance or financial classification would need legal review.

See the parcel as a node.
